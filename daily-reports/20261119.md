## L1 in detail (for the question to Gilad)
**How many validation patients each version draws, per study group and per permutation** (n = that group's training pool):
- **Old:** `max(1, round(n × val_size / 100))`, capped at **n − 1**. A group with ≤ 1 patient gives 0.
- **New (now):** `max(1, round(n × (val_size / 100)))`, capped at **n**.

**1. The cap: only matters for tiny groups or high percentages.** At `val_size: 20`:

| n | Old (n − 1) | New (n) | Training left (old / new) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | **1 / 0** |
| 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 / 1 |
| 40 | 8 | 8 | 32 / 32 |

**Example:** a holdout on one small site, with NOT_LC = 30 patients and LC = 1 patient (P7), `val_size: 20`, 100 permutations.
- **New (cap n):** P7 is validated on every permutation, so training has **no LC patient**. The model is fitted on one class and falls back to a constant prediction at 0.5 (since W1, with a warning). All 100 permutations give a useless model, and the run still looks completed.
- **Old (cap n − 1):** P7 stays in training, so the model is real. Validation has no LC patient, so LC-side metrics (sensitivity, AUC, a fitted threshold) can't be computed.
- **Trade-off:** neither option gives a meaningful result for a one-patient group. The old cap fails more gently (a valid model, incomplete validation). **Recommendation:** `n − 1`, and possibly a warning or a stop when a group has fewer than 2 training-pool patients.

**2. Rounding:** `n × 35 / 100` and `n × 0.35` can round differently (35% of 90: old 32, new 31). Nothing breaks, but old runs with such percentages don't reproduce, and the shared random stream shifts every later permutation. The shipped 20% and 11.1111% aren't affected.

**Question for Gilad:**
1. Go back to the `n − 1` cap, and maybe warn or stop below 2 patients per group?
2. Match the old rounding?

Either change alters draws only in these edge cases, so it goes into W6 with a before/after comparison.
